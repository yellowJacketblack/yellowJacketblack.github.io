GIRL, the Butler — Intelligent Market Listening & ROI Dashboard

What is GIRL, the Butler?

Imagine you have a highly intelligent robot assistant who sits inside your computer, reads the internet all day, and tells you exactly what to buy, when to sell, and how much money you made. That is GIRL, the Butler.

At its core, GIRL is a private Business Intelligence (BI) dashboard that tracks web mentions and uses social listening tools to find hidden trends. Right now, the dashboard is custom-built to help independent vendors resell designer bags (like Coach, Goyard, and Alo). It tells them which bags people are searching for, alerts them on their phones to buy inventory, tracks their sales, and calculates their exact profit margins every week.

A Versatile Analytical Framework

While GIRL currently focuses on luxury handbags, the true power of this project is its analytical bone structure. The underlying architecture is industry agnostic. Because the system relies on tracking keywords, search volume signals, and financial inputs, you can take the exact same codebase and swap out "designer bags" for "vintage sneakers," "indie video games," "crypto tokens," or "real estate flipping." By simply changing the dictionary of words the system looks for, the entire dashboard adapts, tracking popularity, predicting demand, and calculating ROI for literally any market where people search online and sell for a profit.

How the Mechanics Work

GIRL, the Butler actively processes raw information through a machine learning pipeline to generate actionable insights. Here is how the math works behind the scenes:

Data Ingestion & Social Listening

As the Administrator, I feed the system data from two main sources: Google Trends CSV uploads and AnswerThePublic query responses. The system scans these files to find out what people are asking about, what keywords are "Rising," and what is "Declining."

Text Analysis & Machine Learning

Once the data is in the system, automated scripts process the text using several mathematical models, Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency, Named Entity Recognition, and Intent Classification. To predict where the market is going, the dashboard runs Compound Annual Growth Rate, Linear Regression, Pearson Correlation Matrix, and Peak Detection on the search volume data over time.

The Friday Financial Report (ROI)

Every Friday, the system generates a comprehensive financial report. It cross references the strategies the system suggested against the inventory the vendor actually bought and sold. It calculates the time gap between a suggestion and a sale, computes net profit, and even generates a dynamic, common narrative explaining who benefited most from the week's ROI, the vendor or the administrator.

The Dashboard Experience

The platform is built with a strict, role based access system to ensure data privacy and clear workflows:

The Administrator (Me): I input the raw data, run the analysis pipelines, and explain the returns. I act as the data steward, ensuring the machine learning models have fresh information to process.

The Seller: Vendors log in to a secure portal to report their sales, view their Friday financial reports, and see which keywords they should target next.

The Viewer: Clients or investors can log in to a read only portal to view the beautiful, interactive charts (popularity rankings, brand comparisons, emerging vs. declining keywords) to verify the system's effectiveness.

KBCS Radio — Data Analyst

I joined KBCS, Bellevue College's student-run radio station, in January 2023 as a Content Producer for the morning public affairs program — researching stories, summarizing articles into broadcast scripts, and digitally editing audio for featured segments. What was meant to be a short assignment became a three-year progression through the station when, between scripts, I found myself in the office of our Operations & Traffic Manager, Greg Delia, asking how I could assist. What followed was an introduction to WordPress content metadata: the practice of correctly identifying and labeling station content so search engines and listeners could index and retrieve what we produced. Greg's patience in that first conversation redirected the entire trajectory of my work.

Not long after, I found myself working alongside Greg and our station engineers on projects to update our broadcasting software, monitoring and managing metadata for both the KBCS.fm website and our companion mobile app in the capacity of Operations & Traffic Manager's Assistant. In that role, I saw for the first time how a station's entire operational history lived inside relational tables — donor records, broadcast logs, event data — and how much of it existed undocumented and unstructured.

After working beside Greg for less than a year, the volume of the team's seven-day-a-week commitment became unsustainable, and I encouraged Greg and the wider team to consider opening project-based positions for my Bellevue College classmates pursuing the same degree. He agreed two years later: once I was formally admitted to Bellevue College's Bachelor of Applied Science in Data Management & Analysis program, I could intern as a Data Analyst for KBCS. That formalized role commenced in January 2025 — the same quarter my Bellevue College internship coursework (EXPRL 490, Data Analytics & BI Intern) began, earning me academic credit for every deliverable.

My first project was a Python-based data pipeline to extract, categorize, and analyze the station's full backlink profile. The initial script processed 2,747 records and flagged 346 broken links, each requiring individual error handling and retry logic. After Greg had walked me through what those links meant for our domain's search engine ranking — the same principles he had explained in that first office conversation two years prior — I built a second script that classified each broken link by content type: blog, streaming service, local news. The consolidated report informed a targeted outreach campaign that contributed to an 80% improvement in our domain ranking. His original investment made all of that possible.

My next project was an interactive R Shiny dashboard pulling audience engagement data and integrating multi-source geospatial and demographic datasets to surface listening-area patterns. After the application was approved, I migrated it to a production Python and SQL environment on the Hex Platform, where its collaborative, notebook-based interface supported both iterative feature development and stakeholder-facing data storytelling. I served as the point of contact for that feedback — translating the SQL and Python logic behind each chart into language our Development Director and Music Director could act on — and coordinated with the KBCS Web and Operations teams and Bellevue College IT to ensure every component ran cleanly against our internal systems.

Before that dashboard shipped, however, I had encountered NIST — the National Institute of Standards and Technology — in coursework, and something in the data classification and sensitivity framework stayed with me long after the assignment was graded. Before graduation, I made it my mission to document everything: every component of the R Shiny application, the full PostgreSQL schema, the server IP logs, the survey CSV files, the Geo Chart source data, all credentials, and every API key. Then I classified each dataset by sensitivity level — personally identifiable information, moderate risk, low risk — and wrote an authentication access process with role-based access control around them. I was not yet far enough in my degree to understand how JavaScript could have extended that into multi-factor authentication (MFA) and session token management, but the structural foundation was in place.

That documentation work caught the attention of my Program Chair, the KBCS Development Director, our Music Director, and Greg, who all encouraged me to keep building beyond coursework. So the summer of 2025 became a dedicated study period — and an opportunity that extended well past the classroom.

That summer, I was in Bangkok, Thailand, spending weeks inside foreign radio broadcasting stations, learning how audio content was packaged, encoded, and distributed across national and regional networks in languages and markets unlike anything at KBCS.

Before my journey through Bellevue College, my career always scratched the surface of systems. I built foundational skills in account management, process improvement, and logistics data systems through roles at AT&T, Cricket, Home Depot, and Planet Shippers. However, WorkMarket/ADP offered me the position as a remote technician, traveling and troubleshooting technical systems, that ignited my devotion to study beyond the jack of all trades.
